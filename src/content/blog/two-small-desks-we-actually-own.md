If your "home office" is a corner of your bedroom or a nook in a studio apartment,
most desk roundups aren't written for you. Rather than guess at products I've
never touched, here's a genuine comparison of two small desks I've actually
bought and used in my own home.

## What to look for in a small desk

- **Width under 40"** — anything bigger eats into walking space in a small room
- **Wall-mounted or floating options** if you need the floor clear for other furniture
- **Built-in cable management** — small rooms make visible cable mess more obvious
- **Depth over 20"** — narrow desks save width but a monitor plus keyboard still needs real depth

## The one that grows with him: Wayfair adjustable solid wood desk

This is the desk I bought for my son — a 60cm wide, height-adjustable solid
wood computer desk, £29.98 from Wayfair.

What sold me on it was the adjustability. Kids grow fast, and a fixed-height
desk becomes wrong within a year or two. This one adjusts as he does, so it's
not something we'll be replacing every school year. It tucks neatly into the
foot-of-bed space in his room — genuinely useful if you're working with an
awkward small-room layout rather than a proper study.

It's sturdier than the price suggests, and there's enough surface space for
schoolwork without feeling cramped. For the price, this is the one I'd point
another parent toward first.

## FAQ

**Does a small desk mean I can't fit a dual-monitor setup?**
Not necessarily — a monitor arm frees up desk depth that a stand would otherwise use.

**What's the minimum width for a comfortable typing position?**
Around 30 inches is the practical floor for keyboard plus mouse without feeling cramped.
